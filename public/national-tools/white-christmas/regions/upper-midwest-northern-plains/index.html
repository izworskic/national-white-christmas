White Christmas in the Upper Midwest

Across the Upper Midwest and northern Plains, persistent continental cold can make snowpack durability as important as fresh snowfall. Once snow establishes, it can survive for long stretches.

What drives Christmas snow here?

Continental cold

Long periods below freezing can preserve even modest snow depth through Christmas.

Open-terrain wind

Wind can redistribute snow, leaving exposed areas thinner and sheltered areas deeper.

Storm-track timing

A dry December can reduce snow depth even in cold places; a single pre-Christmas system can quickly raise the odds.

Why local matters: Cold does not automatically mean deep snow. The key combination is cold enough to preserve cover plus enough early-winter precipitation to establish it.
